# Design System: Lanyard & Badge

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Badge at the Fair"**

Recruiters meet this engineer the way they meet candidates at a career fair: by reading the badge. The page is sewn from two woven lanyard colourways, navy and concrete, panel to panel, with a stitched seam at every join; everything readable is printed on off-white PVC badge stock in near-black ink, hung from silver clip metal. Project screenshots are die-cut stickers with white kiss-cut borders slapped onto the webbing; metrics hang off them on die-cut tags tied with leader lines; achievements are photo passes hanging from a navy strap that rides slowly past (slowing under the cursor), each pass turning through up to four photos, rank told by the cut of a tag; activities are the event agenda; experience is the itinerary. One vertical cord runs down the left of the page and every section clips onto it.

The system is physical, not digital. Depth comes from objects lying on fabric (neutral dark drop shadows under hung or stuck things), never from glow, gloss, or a metallic sheen. State is told by form (a tag's cut, a sticker lifting) and printed words, never by hue alone. The only saturated surfaces are the two webbings themselves; content never gets its own accent colour. One type family, Archivo variable, does everything by moving along its width axis: expanded black for names and display, normal width for reading, condensed caps for printed labels.

This file is derived from the shipped mockup (`mockup/index.html`, `mockup/styles.css`, `mockup/main.js`, `mockup/cv.html`). It is the spec for the production build (Next.js + SQLite, public `/`, `/projects/[slug]`, `/cv`, plus `/admin`). The owner's name, portrait, and GitHub account are real; every other name, metric, screenshot, and achievement in the mockup is synthetic and must be replaced (see the content checklist in Do's and Don'ts).

The two-colourway world replaced the original single ultramarine ground on 2026-11-14 at the owner's request (the ultramarine read as too loud). The home page and the CV carry the revision. The case study page has not been migrated yet (see below).

The case study page (`mockup/project.html`, `mockup/project.css`, `mockup/project.js`, on top of the shared `mockup/styles.css`; production `/projects/[slug]`) extends the world without changing it. It reads as an event program held on the lanyard: the header sits on the ground, a sticky program index on the left tells you where you are, each part of the story is printed on its own stock page, the architecture is a set of booths on a strip of deep twill wired together with stock cords, and the results hang as ink tags from a webbing rail. Its rules are recorded in each section below next to the home page's. **Pending migration:** `mockup/project.html` and `mockup/project.css` were built for the single ultramarine ground and have not been moved to the two-colourway world. Today the page inherits the navy defaults from the shared stylesheet as one flat panel without the twill (the page-wide weave layer is gone), and its compact index bar still uses the ultramarine-era `rgba(26, 39, 173, 0.94)`. Its rules below describe its structure; where they name a ground colour, read it as navy until the page is migrated and given its own panel sequence.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Two woven webbing colourways, navy and concrete, alternating section by section with the raster twill on every panel and a stitched seam at every join.
- Content on off-white badge stock in near-black ink; 18px card corners, 16px sticker corners, full pills for buttons.
- One family, Archivo variable (wdth 62 to 125, wght 100 to 900), roles separated by width as much as weight.
- Physical motion: a damped pendulum badge on a concrete strap with a metal ring and lobster clasp, stickers that slap down and then drift and lean with the pointer over a field of punched dust, leader lines that draw to the result.
- State by form and printed word, never by colour alone.
- Every creative moment has a plain readable path; reduced motion renders every section in its final state.
- Case studies read as an event program: a sticky index with a printed "Reading" tag, one stock page per section, the cord as reading progress.

## Colors

Two webbing colourways (navy and concrete) and a press palette of stock, ink, and flat metal; one live green dot; nothing else.

### Primary: the two webbings
- **Webbing Navy** (`webbing-navy`): the dark colourway. The ground of the hero, work, activities, and closing panels; the badge's header band; the CV's strap band, foot, cord, section tabs, header rule, and metric figures; the print on the concrete strap; the achievements reel strap and each pass's short strap (webbing tile over navy). On the CV it is also the text-selection colour.
- **Webbing Navy Deep** (`webbing-navy-deep`): the shadowed navy weave. Punched slots on the navy panels (the ground showing through), the solid nav at 0.94 alpha (`rgba(21, 28, 49, 0.94)`, also the CV toolbar), the scrollbar track, and the CV slot.
- **Webbing Concrete** (`webbing-concrete`): the light colourway. ### Named Rules
**The Two Colourway Rule.** The page is sewn from two webbings and nothing else: navy and concrete alternate panel by panel in story order (hero navy, about concrete, work navy, achievements concrete, activities navy, experience concrete, closing navy), every join carries the stitched seam, and a panel never changes colourway partway through. Webbing colours are grounds, straps, and printed bands; they never become a text highlight on stock. On stock and paper navy appears only as print: the badge's header band, the CV's strap, cord, tabs, rule, and metric figures.

**The State By Form Rule.** Rank, role, and status are told by cut, length, and the printed word. Two items that differ only by hue are a defect.

**The Press Palette Rule.** Two webbings, stock, white, ink, flat metal. A new colour needs a physical reason (it is a material on a badge or lanyard), or it does not ship. The live green dot is the one standing exception.

### Named Rules
**The Width Axis Rule.** Hierarchy moves on `wdth` as much as on weight: names and display at 118 to 125, reading at 96 to 100, printed labels at 72 to 85. Never fake width with `transform: scaleX` or letter-spacing.

**The Tabular Numbers Rule.** Any figure the eye compares (metrics, years, dates, GPA, latency) sets `font-feature-settings: "tnum" 1`.

**The Printed Label Rule.** Uppercase tracked condensed caps exist only as print on a physical object (rank tag, role tag, itinerary list label, strap, seal, the badge's header band, and on the CV the section tabs, cut tags, and dates). They never float above a heading as a section label.

**The Code Is Data Rule.** Identifiers in prose (`SKIP LOCKED`) stay in Archivo at 700, wdth 85, on a 7% ink wash with `label-cut` corners. Monospace never appears, not even for code.

The three layers each own one kind of movement so none fights another: GSAP's entrance writes `transform` on the slot (and clears it); the mouse parallax writes `--px` and `--py`, which the slot applies through the individual `translate` property, never `transform`; the idle drift is the `float` keyframes on the inner span. ## Elevation & Depth

Depth is physical: objects hang over or lie on woven fabric and cast neutral, near-black shadows (`rgba(10, 11, 18, a)`). Shadows are always offset downward with a negative spread so they read as a thing a few centimetres off the cloth. The same shadow is used on both colourways; on concrete it reads lighter, and that is correct. There is no glow, no coloured or tinted halo, no inner gloss, no gradient sheen. The hanging badge is the one object allowed to cast across a seam.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Hang** (`0 28px 60px -18px rgba(10,11,18,.62), 0 6px 14px -6px rgba(10,11,18,.4)`): anything hanging or lying flat as a card: badge faces, project cards, itinerary. Achievement passes use the Sticker shadow at rest and Lift on hover.
- **Sticker** (`0 14px 28px -10px rgba(10,11,18,.55), 0 2px 4px rgba(10,11,18,.25)`): stickers at rest, chips, caption-scale objects.
- **Lift** (`0 30px 50px -16px rgba(10,11,18,.7), 0 6px 12px -4px rgba(10,11,18,.35)`): a peeled sticker and the agenda photo preview.

### Case study depth

### Named Rules
**The Neutral Shadow Rule.** Every shadow is near-black and offset down. A shadow tinted with the ground, the field colour, or white is a glow and is banned.

**The Hole Not Light Rule.** Insets are only for things cut into material (slots, eyelets, webbing edges). Never use an inset as a highlight or rim light.

### Hero dust (canvas)
- A `canvas.hero-dust` fills the hero (`inset: 0`, z-index 1, `pointer-events: none`, `aria-hidden`), behind the statement, stickers, and lanyard. It is a decoration of the punched ground: the specks a hole punch leaves. It carries no copy.
- **Specks:** dots in stock `242,242,238` (twice as likely), concrete `229,228,223`, and 12% slot-shaped chads (6.4x2px, radius 1, in `201,204,214`) that slowly spin. Count is the hero area plus the lean margin divided by 7200, capped at 300 (about one speck per 85px square); they are scattered 150px past the left and right edges and 90px past the top and bottom, so leaning never uncovers a bare strip. Each has a depth `z` of 0.15 to 1: radius `0.6 + 1.5 z` px, alpha `0.18 + 0.42 z`, and a slow wander of up to 3px/s on each axis, wrapping at the margins.
- **Lean:** fine pointers only. The pointer position across the hero maps to -1..1 on each axis, the field eases toward it (rate `2.2 / s`, so it trails the cursor), and each speck shifts by `150px * z` on x and `90px * z` on y in the direction of the pointer: near specks travel further than far ones, which reads as depth. Leaving the hero eases the field home. The hero's page box is measured with its size (on resize), and a move compares the pointer's page position against it, so no pointer move forces a layout.
- **Idle and cost:** it has its own frame loop (dt capped at 50ms), and schedules no frame at all while the hero is off screen (IntersectionObserver) or the tab is hidden; both start it again. Device pixel ratio is capped at 2. It reseeds only when the hero changes by more than 40px in either axis.
- **Reduced motion:** drawn once, still, and redrawn only when the hero resizes; no loop, no lean.
- **Fails alone:** it is decoration, so it runs inside its own guard: if anything in it throws, the canvas is left empty and the rest of the home motion (reel, agenda, nav, entrance) runs on. A chad is drawn with `roundRect`, or a plain `rect` where a browser the build still serves has none (Firefox before 112, Safari before 16).

### Agent swarm (a crew of cursors per section builds it, then leaves)
Owner's request, 2026-11-17, after Swarm Rebuild (a tribute to Joel Califa's *Swarm*): the portfolio is about AI agents, so on every visit a crew of agents builds the page in front of the visitor the way a team builds a page in a design tool: boxes dragged out, text typed, parts copied and then edited. Our own engine (`components/home/swarm/`); the reference has no license, so only the idea and the design-tool vocabulary are taken.
- **When.** Every load of the home page, a reload too (the owner's choice, 2026-11-17; a sessionStorage flag `swarm-built` that only tests set skips it). The nav is not built: it is there from the first paint. A head script holds the rest of the page before first paint (`html.swarm-pending`: section grounds show, their contents wait); the swarm then hides each part in place itself (`.sw-hide`, visibility, so nothing shifts). If the script never runs, the parts show after 4s. Escape puts everything in place at once.
- **Agents.** A design tool's pointer (arrow, I-beam, crosshair, pen, rotate, duplicate, resize) in one of the page's own tones, picked for the ground of the section its crew works in (on navy: stock, metal, blue-grey, concrete; on concrete: navy, ink; the live dot's green on both), with a rim that contrasts it, and a printed tag in the same tone with its initials (10.5px/750, tracked 0.08em; 9.5px on phones): two random letters, as people show up in a shared design file, no two alike on the page, never doubled letters, a few pairs that read as words, or the owner's own "JF".
- **Crews.** Each section has its own crew. There is no gathering first: work starts at once, and a crew's agents come in from the side of the screen nearest the work they are wanted for, level with it. The first is alone: the headline's lead line ("AI integrations") is the first thing on the page, and nothing else starts until it is typed and grown. A crew builds only its own section and grows while its work piles up (about one hand per 3.5 tasks, 3 to 14; 2 to 6 on phones), calling in hands only for work that can be taken up right now. A hand with nothing to do stays where it stopped for 0.7s, then heads off the screen; it does not wait at the edges. Hands come back in as work opens, as the visitor scrolls.
- **Marks.** Drawn by the ground under them: stock on navy, ink on concrete; selections are a 1.5px rule with four handles and a size badge (`W × H`, tabular), placeholders are metal grey (an image glyph for pictures), text boxes a dashed hairline, carets 2px. Copies and gaps use the live dot's green (`--sw-accent`; mixed toward ink on concrete). No rulers and no guides (the owner found them noisy).
- **How a part is built.** A box or picture: dragged out with the crosshair over a placeholder, then filled (pictures sweep in from the top). Text: a text box dragged over its first line, typed key by key under the I-beam (a clip that follows the caret); headings are typed at 18px and grown a size at a time. A label (button, chip, tag, rank): its box, then its words typed in. A tilted part is built upright and turned into its tilt with the rotate handle (its angle on the badge). Lines (lanyard straps, leader lines, rules) are drawn with the pen, anchor by anchor. A result tag's number counts up once clicked into; the tag swings onto its line when complete; the badge swings once all of it is in place.
- **Copies.** Alike parts (the headline's lines, buttons, chips, tags, rows, passes, stops) are built once, then copied: the nearest built one is selected, dragged out with the duplicate key held (dashed green outlines, the gap measured in a green badge) into its place, where it stands in with the master's words and pictures; agents then edit it: a double click selects the old words (green highlight), the new ones are typed over (a text over 36 characters is pasted instead: it lands at once, highlighted), pictures are swapped (the old wipes away from the top; an icon in a line of words pops in once the words are retyped). At most three copies of a set are dragged at once, so they cascade.
- **Each section its own way.** Nav: not built. Hero: the lead line ("AI integrations,", the one about AI) typed small and grown, alone; then copied into the lines above and below it, each copy retyped ("Web apps and", "from first idea", "to launch"), the headline's work going ahead of anything else in the hero (on a phone the badge sits above it); meanwhile screens dropped in as image boxes and turned into their tilt; one button built, the other copied. Not built, by the owner's choice (2026-11-17), so the page is there sooner: the badge (it drops in on its lanyard by itself and swings), the skill chips (they pop in by themselves as they scroll in), the work cards (their leader lines draw on and result tags swing in and count up as they scroll in), the achievement passes (the strip keeps running), the activities agenda, and everything in the closing but its heading and wave. Those sections build only their headings and icon stickers. About: several agents type the paragraph at once, a line each. Experience: the card drawn, the first stop typed, the rest copied down. Closing: "Let's talk." grows a size at a time and its wave is dropped in.
- **Pace.** The build runs at 1.6x a person's pace; About at 1.4x that, Work at 1.5x and Achievements at 2.2x, their repeated parts being the most. Work unlocks top down as the visitor scrolls (to 1.2 screens below the top); a part on screen also pulls in everything it waits for, wherever that is, so a copy never sits waiting on its master's lower half below the fold. A part may start as soon as the box it sits in shows. Work left far above is put in place at once, so nothing is missing on the way back. While the page is built the floating screens hold still and the nav stays shown.
- **After.** Nobody stays (the owner's choice, 2026-11-17, replacing the parked corner huddles): once its section is built, the whole crew dashes off the nearest edge of the screen (a side or the bottom) together, speeding up as they go, so the section is clear in about half a second. With no one left, nothing comes over to what the visitor points at.
- **Sound.** Synthesized live (Web Audio, no files): mouse presses, mechanical key ticks while typing, a crisp snap as a tilted part lands on its angle, rising ticks as a heading grows, and a sparse glass-piano note from the E major pentatonic for each part set in place, on an eighth-note grid (big parts low, small high, rising left to right, panned); a chord when the page is built. Silent after that. Browsers allow sound only after a first click or key. The only control is the sound switch in the nav (a 42px outlined circle with a speaker; waves faded while sound waits for that first click, crossed out when off); the visitor's choice is remembered.
- **Reduced motion:** no swarm, no hold, no sound switch.

### Deliberate detector exceptions
These trip automated design checks on purpose and are part of the system:
- **Nested cards:** architecture booths are cards inside the architecture field inside a stock page. The field is a strip of fabric laid on the sheet and the booths are objects on that fabric, so the nesting is physical, not decorative.
- **Display headings at line-height 1.0:** page headlines and program index rows set `line-height: 1` (and the larger display tiers go below 1). Tight leading on condensed or expanded display type is intended; running paragraphs never go under 1.45.
- **Per-project field colour:** the next-project card's field carries another project's colour, a value outside the palette tokens. It is record data, contained to the field, as on project cards.
- **Marquee (achievements reel):** the detector's marquee rule is ignored for `mockup/index.html` in `.impeccable/config.json` because the owner asked for this motion. For `mockup/project.html` the same ignore is a false positive: it loads the shared `styles.css` but has no reel element.
